# Module 1 — Prospecting

> Goal: book qualified first meetings consistently across phone, email, LinkedIn, and video.
> Tags: **📌 30MPC** = sourced doctrine · **🛠 RAMPED** = our build on top of it.

---

## 1.1 Mindset and math

- 📌 30MPC's flagship claim: their cold-call script books **1 meeting out of every 3 connects**
  (#489, #540). Jason Bay on 30MPC: the best callers he's seen hit ~1 in 3 connects *because they
  call people they already know are a good fit and did research first*. The average positive-outcome
  rate is closer to ~1%.
- 📌 Objections are where the call starts: *"once you get past the first 30–45 seconds, objections are
  where the call really begins"* (30MPC × Orum guide).
- 📌 First objections are usually a reflex to being interrupted, not a reaction to your pitch
  (*Cold Calling Sucks* book notes).

🛠 **RAMPED activity targets (starting point; calibrate after 30 days of data)**

| Metric | SDR | AE (self-sourced) |
|---|---|---|
| Dial blocks / day | 2 × 60 min | 1 × 60 min |
| Dials / block | ~40 | ~40 |
| New accounts researched / week | 50 | 20 |
| Active sequenced prospects | 150–250 | 50–100 |
| Meetings booked / week | 4–6 | 2–3 |

## 1.2 The cold call: Opener → Pitch → Objections

📌 The 30MPC call has three parts: **the opener, the pitch (a "problem proposition"), and objection
handling** using the **Mr. Miyagi** framework. The goal is to sell the meeting, not the product.

### Prep: research in blocks, dial in blocks

- 📌 *"Get all your research done before, 40 dials over 60 minutes"* (Playbook 1).
- 📌 Cap prep at ~2 minutes per prospect to find a single hook (Morgan Ingram, Club Playbook #173).
- 🛠 For each call block, prep a list of 40 with these columns: Name · Title · Persona · Hook
  (one line) · Peer logos to mention · Problem you'll lead with.

### Openers

📌 **Opener A, "Heard the name tossed around".** Ranked #1 of the openers tested in Gong's
300M-call study written by Armand: ~11.2% success. It works because it makes a cold call feel like a
warm intro. Lead with peers or competitors you work with, then ask assumptively.

🛠 RAMPED talk track:
> "Hey {First}, it's {Me} from RAMPED — we work with {Peer 1} and {Peer 2}. Have you heard our name
> tossed around?"
>
> *(Either answer:)* "No worries — quick context, then you can tell me if it's relevant…" → Pitch.

📌 **Opener B, the permission-based opener.** About 11.2% success. It works because it *"owns that
you're making a cold call and disarms the prospect with brutal honesty."* Three steps:
1. Lead with context.
2. Own the cold call.
3. Get permission to pitch.

🛠 RAMPED talk track:
> "Hey {First}, {Me} from RAMPED. I saw {hook — e.g., you're hiring three {role}s}. Full honesty,
> this is a cold call — can I take 30 seconds to tell you why I called, and you can tell me if it's
> worth keeping talking?"

📌 **Openers to stop using**
- *"Did I catch you at a bad time?"* performs roughly 40% worse than baseline.
- *"Could I get 30 seconds to tell you why I called?"* used on its own, with no context, sounds like
  every telemarketer.

📌 **Tone.** A warm voice is hard to hang up on (Jason Bay). Morgan Ingram: thank them for picking
up, then use a pattern interrupt along the lines of "we don't know each other — here's why I'm calling."

📌 **Be disarmingly blunt.** If they ask how you got their number: *"On the internet"* or *"In a
database."* Don't dodge it.

### Pitch: the problem proposition

📌 Rules:
- Spend most of the pitch on the **problem**, described vividly.
- Give the differentiator in **one sentence**.
- Don't put yourself in a product category.
- No buzzwords.
- The aim is to create curiosity, not to trigger objections.

🛠 RAMPED pitch template:
> "I work with {persona} at {peer-type companies}. They usually tell me one of two things:
> either {problem 1, vivid, in their words} — or {problem 2, vivid}.
> We {one-sentence differentiator}.
> Are either of those on your radar?"

🛠 Fill one of these in for every persona in the Messaging Matrix (§1.5). Ban these words from the
pitch: *platform, solution, leverage, streamline, best-in-class, AI-powered.*

### Close for the meeting

- 📌 Use an interest-based CTA, not a big ask.
- 📌 When the fit is clear, be assumptive and roll straight into scheduling (Jason Bay).
- 📌 Suggest specific times, or send a placeholder invite while you're still on the phone (Playbook 12).

🛠 Example:
> "Sounds like {problem} is real for you. Worth 30 minutes to see how {peer} handled it? I've got
> Tuesday at 10 or Wednesday at 2 — I'll send a placeholder now and you can move it."

### Objections: the Mr. Miyagi framework

📌 Use one framework for every objection. Never fight it; flow with it.

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0. Pause | Wait ~1 second so they finish (Morgan Ingram script) |
| 1. Acknowledge | Agree with the feeling, not the conclusion |
| 2. Clarify | Ask a question that surfaces the *real* reason |
| 3. Wax on / wax off | Return to the problem proposition, now tailored |

- 📌 **"Not interested" is a brush-off.** Stop at the acknowledge step, let the real objection come
  out, then run the framework on that.
- 📌 **Three objection types.** Gong's data via 30MPC puts them at:
  - Dismissive, ~50% (e.g., "not interested", "send me an email")
  - Situational, ~43% (e.g., "no budget", "call me in 6 months")
  - Existing solution, ~8% (e.g., "we use X")
- 📌 The 30MPC × Orum guide covers the five objections below. 30MPC also publishes an
  "18 cold call objections" guide (see [Sources](SOURCES.md#open-gaps)).

🛠 **RAMPED talk tracks.** These are our wording, built on the Miyagi steps.

| Objection | Acknowledge | Clarify | Wax back |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Not interested** | "Totally fair — you didn't wake up hoping I'd call." | "Out of curiosity, is it that {problem} isn't a thing for you, or just bad timing?" | Re-run the pitch on whichever they pick |
| **Send me an email** | "Happy to." | "So I don't send you a novel — which was closer, {problem 1} or {problem 2}?" | "Got it. Most {persona}s want to see how {peer} fixed that — let's put 20 min on the calendar and I'll send the email as the agenda." |
| **No budget** | "Makes sense — nobody has budget for something they haven't seen yet." | "Is it that nothing's allocated this year, or that {problem} isn't a priority right now?" | "If {problem} is costing {impact}, worth 20 min to see if it pays for itself before planning season?" |
| **Call me in 6 months** | "Can do." | "What changes in 6 months — new budget, a project wrapping, something else?" | "Let's do 15 min now so you've got the info when that hits, and I'll put the 6-month follow-up on the calendar too." |
| **We already use {X}** | "Good — {X} is solid, and a lot of our customers came from {X}." | "If you could wave a wand and change one thing about it, what would it be?" | Tie their answer to the differentiator → ask for the meeting |
| **How'd you get my number?** | — | "On the internet." *(smile)* | Back to the pitch |

### Gatekeepers and voicemail

- 📌 Gatekeepers: #497 teaches three tactics for getting past any gatekeeper *"without lying, pitching,
  or wasting time."*
- 🛠 Our default with a gatekeeper:
  - Be brief, confident, and use first names: "Hey, it's {Me} for {First}."
  - If they ask "what's it regarding?", say: "It's about {problem area} — {First} will know if it's relevant."
  - Never fake familiarity.
- 📌 Voicemail: use a *"one-two punch of voicemails that lead with context and then point the prospect
  to email"* (Playbook 12). Make voicemails and emails reference each other (Kyle Coleman). Keep them short.
- 🛠 Voicemail script (under 20 seconds, never ask for a callback):
  > "{First}, {Me} at RAMPED. Calling because {peer} was dealing with {problem}. I'm sending you an
  > email with the subject '{subject}' — take a look there. Thanks."

## 1.3 Cold email

### Principles

- 📌 One pain point per email; don't sell everything (Playbook 7).
- 📌 Under 125 words. If you have to scroll more than once, fix it (Kyle Coleman).
- 📌 Subject lines:
  - 1–3 words (Anthony Natoli), or
  - Sam McKenna's style: *"a super funky subject line that makes absolutely no sense except to the
    person that's receiving it."*
- 📌 **No calendar links** in cold outbound emails (Sam McKenna, #596).
- 📌 Use an **interest-based CTA** on first touches ("Open to learning more?"). It beats a meeting ask in
  Gong's data. Switch to specific times once they're in a deal. No ROI claims in a cold email.
- 📌 "Show Me You Know Me" (#samsales): open with the research, not a generic check-in. Pre-empt the
  likely objection inside the email. Move from rapport into the pitch.
- 📌 Write like a text to a friend; formality isn't the same thing as professionalism.
- 📌 The 5×5×5 research habit: 5 minutes of research → 5 insights → write it in 5 minutes.

### 🛠 RAMPED email templates

**Email 1: Show Me You Know Me + Problem**
```
Subject: {2–3 words only they'd get, e.g. "Austin SOC hires"}

{First} — {specific observation: their post / hire / initiative / news} caught my eye.

Usually when {persona}s are {doing that}, {problem in their words} shows up fast —
{one concrete symptom}.

{Peer} was in the same spot; we {one-line outcome}.

Worth a look, or not a priority right now?

{Me}
```

**Email 2: Second pain (new thread)**
```
Subject: {problem 2 in 2 words}

{First} — different angle. Most {persona}s I talk to at {peer-type} say {problem 2}.
{One sentence on why it's costly}. We {differentiator}.

Is that on your radar this quarter?
```

**Email 3: Peer proof**
```
Subject: {peer name}

{First} — {peer}'s {title} was dealing with {problem}. In {timeframe} they {result}.
Happy to share what they changed, even if we never work together. Interested?
```

**Breakup email**
```
Subject: closing the loop

{First} — haven't heard back, so I'll assume {problem} isn't a priority right now.
If that changes, reply "later" and I'll check back next quarter. Either way, appreciate you.
```

### Personalization at scale

- 📌 Personalize by persona first, then add one trigger. Jason Bay's order: Messaging Matrix →
  real personalization trigger → write → tighten → create variations.
- 📌 AI can draft from detailed inputs, then a human edits for the personal touch. AI-written emails
  that named specific employees still read as human.
- 🛠 Personalization tiers:

| Tier | Accounts | Personalization |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Top 10% of ICP | ~25 | Full "show me you know me": custom first line + custom video |
| 2 — Strong fit | ~100 | Persona template + one trigger line |
| 3 — Fit | Rest | Persona template only |

## 1.4 The outbound sequence

📌 Verified rules:
- Start with an email, then **~10 touches over ~30 days**, mixing calls and emails (Playbook 7).
- Open with a 3-touch cluster: personalized email + call + LinkedIn connect (Anthony Natoli).
- Keep it to about 3 sequences for your main personas plus 1 low-priority one; not 3,000 sequences (Sam Nelson).
- Once a meeting is accepted, stop selling and don't send a confirmation pitch.
- Build a separate event sequence for trade shows ("Open to stopping by the booth?").

🛠 **RAMPED Core Persona Sequence (10 touches / 30 days)**

| Day | Touch | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Email 1 + Call (VM 1) + LinkedIn connect (blank or one line) | Pain A, Show-Me-You-Know-Me |
| 3 | Call (VM 2 → "check my email") | Reference Email 1 |
| 5 | Email 2 (new thread) | Pain B |
| 8 | Call | No VM |
| 10 | LinkedIn: engage with their post or send a <45s video | Video formula below |
| 14 | Email 3 | Peer proof |
| 17 | Call + VM | Reference Email 3 |
| 21 | Email 4 | Pain C or a useful insight (no ask) |
| 25 | Call | — |
| 30 | Breakup email | "Reply 'later'" |

🛠 After day 30:
- Recycle the prospect into a quarterly nurture.
- Before re-engaging, redo the research. Don't re-send the same sequence (Natoli).

## 1.5 Messaging Matrix (build this before writing anything)

📌 Jason Bay's matrix: for each persona, map **Priorities → Current solutions → Problems → Aspirations**.

🛠 Fill one out per persona (template in [`04-templates-and-scorecards.md`](04-templates-and-scorecards.md#messaging-matrix)).
- Every pitch, email, and voicemail pulls from this matrix.
- Plan **3 problem statements per persona**, and use **one per touch** (Natoli).

## 1.6 LinkedIn, video, and other channels

- 📌 Don't treat LinkedIn like email. Use connects, engage with their content, and use DMs
  strategically (Playbook 11). Blank connection requests are fine.
- 📌 Prospecting video formula: **under 45 seconds → who you are, why you're reaching out, value + CTA**
  (#531). Send a confirmation video on the day of the meeting to cut no-shows.
- 📌 Use offline touches for Tier 1 accounts, e.g. gifts tied to an occasion. *"Be everywhere — social,
  email, phone call, and mail."*
- 🛠 Video script (about 40 seconds):
  - Hold up a whiteboard with their name, or screen-share their website.
  - "Hi {First}, {Me} from RAMPED. Recorded this because {trigger}. {Peer}s usually hit {problem} —
    here's the 10-second version of how we fix it. If it's relevant, reply and we'll grab 20 minutes."

## 1.7 Territory and account planning

- 📌 Target **lookalikes** of your wins. If one segment converts, go get more accounts like it
  (Luke Floyd, #187).
- 📌 Get to power early: involve decision-makers from the start (Jason Bay).
- 📌 Run prospecting as an **operating rhythm**: set daily expectations, put them on the calendar
  as invites, and get "wildly tactical" (Shay Keeler, #183).
- 🛠 Quarterly territory plan:
  1. Export your book and tier it 1/2/3 by ICP fit plus a trigger signal.
  2. Pick the top 25 Tier 1 accounts. Map 3–5 personas for each.
  3. Find your 3 best closed-won deals. Build a lookalike list of 50 from them.
  4. Block your calendar: daily dial blocks, a weekly research block, a weekly Tier 1 video block.
  5. Review the plan monthly with your manager. Promote and demote accounts based on engagement.

## 1.8 Prospecting do's and don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Research, then dial in a 60-minute block | Research between every dial |
| Open with context ("heard the name…" / permission) | Ask "Did I catch you at a bad time?" |
| Pitch the problem; give a one-line differentiator | Use buzzwords or name your product category |
| Treat "not interested" as a brush-off | Argue with an objection |
| Keep emails under 125 words with one pain each | Put calendar links in cold emails |
| Use interest CTAs early, specific times later | Ask for 30 minutes in the first email |
| Point voicemails to email | Ask for a callback |
| Stop selling once the meeting is booked | Keep pitching after "yes" |
