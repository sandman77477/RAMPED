# Module 2 — Closing

> Goal: turn first meetings into signed deals predictably. Stages are defined by what the buyer has
> agreed to, not by which meetings happened.
> Tags: **📌 30MPC** = sourced doctrine · **🛠 RAMPED** = our build on top of it.

---

## 2.1 The 5-Stage Sales Process (buyer agreements)

📌 Mark Kosoglow's *"$250M ARR 5-stage sales process"* (30MPC Playbook, Sell, Jan 2025):
- Stop running stages based on meetings held. Use stages based on **agreements** the buyer has made.
- This keeps deals from stalling late.
- To apply it: *"Map these stages to your sales cycle, define clear exit criteria for each, and
  identify opportunities to combine calls or stages to accelerate deal velocity."*

📌 Public listings give two versions of the stage names, so both are shown. Confirm against the
gated 30MPC stage template before you finalize.

| # | Version A (2025 episode description) | Version B (later summaries) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem Agreement | Problem Agreement |
| 2 | Solution Agreement | Priority Agreement |
| 3 | Power Agreement | Evaluation Agreement |
| 4 | Commercial Agreement | Value Agreement |
| 5 | Vendor Approval | Commercial Agreement |

🛠 **RAMPED stages and exit criteria.** We merged the two versions. Each stage is a CRM stage, and
the exit criteria are required fields.

| CRM stage | Buyer agreement | Exit criteria (all must be true) | Typical calls |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 Problem | "We have this problem." | 1. Buyer has stated the problem in their own words.<br>2. Impact is quantified, even roughly.<br>3. The 5-Minute Drill has been run. | Discovery |
| S2 Priority | "Fixing it matters *now*." | 1. Why-now is documented (event, deadline, cost of waiting).<br>2. Buyer has confirmed it is a top-3 priority this half. | Discovery 2 / exec discovery |
| S3 Power & Evaluation | "The people who decide agree on how we'll decide." | 1. Economic buyer is identified and has been met.<br>2. Evaluation criteria and the evaluation steps are agreed.<br>3. Close plan has been shared. | Demo, technical validation |
| S4 Value | "This is worth the investment." | 1. Business case has been co-written with the champion.<br>2. Economic buyer has agreed to the value. | Business case review |
| S5 Commercial & Vendor Approval | "Here's the price and paper." | 1. Price and terms agreed.<br>2. Security, legal and procurement steps are dated in the close plan. | Negotiation, redlines |
| Closed Won | Signed | — | Kickoff |

🛠 **Combining stages to go faster.** On smaller deals, run S1 and S2 in one discovery. On deals where
the champion has power, run S3 and S4 together.

## 2.2 Running every call

### The three-chapter call

📌 Every call has three chapters:
1. **First 5 minutes:** build credibility and set a **PPO agenda** (Purpose, Plan, Outcome).
2. **Middle:** uncover problems.
3. **Last 5 minutes:** the closing drill.

📌 Playbook 4 ("run a sales process"):
- **Before the call:** send a shared agenda, get standard qualifying questions out of the way early, and research every attendee.
- **During the call:** state what success looks like by the end, and lock in next steps.

🛠 PPO opener:
> "Thanks for the time. **Purpose:** understand how you're handling {area} today and whether
> {problem} is a priority. **Plan:** I'll ask a bunch of questions, share what we're seeing at
> {peers}, and if it makes sense, a quick look at how we approach it. **Outcome:** by the end we'll
> both know if a next step makes sense — and if not, totally fine to say so. Anything you'd add?"

### The 5-Minute Drill

📌 From episode 113 (Armand). In the last five minutes of every call, ask:
1. **"Do you want to buy?"** Do they want to solve this problem?
2. **"When do you want to buy?"** When do they need it solved?
3. **"How do you buy?"** What steps and which people does it take?

📌 Principles on next steps:
- *"Bad: never setting next steps. Okay: setting steps on every deal. **Good: setting steps on REAL deals.**"*
- Suggest the **next-next steps** so the buyer agrees to a sales process, not just a free demo (Jake Dunlap, #102).
- Aim to schedule next steps on at least 70% of your opportunities (Discovery teardown).

🛠 The drill, word for word:
> "We've got five minutes — can I be direct? Based on what you shared, is {problem} something you
> want to fix, or more of a nice-to-have? … When would you need it solved by, and what's driving
> that date? … Walk me through how something like this gets bought at {Company} — the last time you
> bought software like this, who was involved and what were the steps?"

📌 Andy Whyte (MEDDICC, #87): *"Ask about the last time they bought something before asking for the
buying process."*

## 2.3 Discovery

### The Discovery Tree

📌 30MPC's "run discovery for any product" framework. Discovery goes down three levels:

**Situation → Operational problem → Executive / business impact**

- 📌 Use "typically" questions, e.g. *"Typically when we talk to {persona}s, they say X — how does that
  compare?"* Also use "magic moment" questions to bring high-value pain to the surface.
- 📌 Teach reps *where the conversation is going*, not a 57-point checklist. Start with a few problem
  trees and refine them on live calls.

🛠 Example tree. Fill in one per persona.
```
Situation:        How are you handling {area} today? Who owns it? What tools?
 └ Operational:   Typically {persona}s tell us {problem}. How does that show up for you?
                  How often? What happens when it does? What have you tried?
   └ Executive:   Who else feels that? What does it cost — time, $, risk, headcount?
                  If nothing changes in 12 months, what happens?
     └ Impact:    What would it mean for {exec's goal} if it were solved?
```

### The 3×3 for teaching discovery

📌 From "How to Teach Discovery Without Confusing the Hell Out of Your Sales Team" (Lead Playbook, Apr 2025):
- Three phases: **Agenda / Meat / Next Steps**.
- Each phase has three core moves.

🛠 Our nine moves. These are RAMPED wording; the 30MPC original isn't public.

| Agenda | Meat | Next Steps |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Rapport (one human thing) | 4. Situation questions | 7. 5-Minute Drill |
| 2. PPO agenda | 5. Problem → impact (the tree) | 8. Book the next meeting live |
| 3. Ask what they'd add | 6. "Looking for trouble" (what could stop this?) | 9. Agree on the next-next step |

### How to ask questions (Charles Muhlbauer)

- 📌 **Humbling disclaimer.** Ask permission before the hard question, e.g. *"I feel a bit crazy asking this, but…"*
- 📌 **"I'd rather be more accurate than optimistic."** Say this before you test the deal.
- 📌 **"To what extent…"** turns any yes/no question into an open one.
- 📌 **Question roadmap:**
  1. Share context.
  2. Share an insight.
  3. Use a humbling disclaimer.
  4. Ask an open-ended question.
- 📌 Ask **where the problem came from and what they've tried before**, not only how big it is.
- 📌 Keep qualifying questions separate from discovery questions. Discovery should make them *think*.
- 📌 **Look for trouble.** Ask what would stop them moving forward (#106).
- 📌 Use a **menu of pain**: have the 3 biggest problems for each persona ready (Krysten Conner).

🛠 Example using the roadmap:
> "Most {persona}s we work with are running {situation} *(context)*. What we're seeing is that the
> ones who wait until {event} end up {consequence} *(insight)*. I'd rather be accurate than
> optimistic, so — this might sound blunt *(disclaimer)* — **to what extent** is this actually on
> your plate this quarter versus something you'd like to get to?"

## 2.4 Demo

- 📌 **Reset the agenda before you demo:**
  1. Tell them what you'll show.
  2. Show it.
  3. Recap and tie it back to the outcome you agreed at the start.
- 📌 Assume your buyer will have about 60 seconds to pitch you internally (Robert Friedland, #302).
- 📌 **Reverse demo.** The prospect shows their current workflow first, which exposes the real pain (Alex Kane, #539).
- 📌 **Big Demo Deck.** Agree early on what they want to see and why it matters (#539, #113).
- 📌 Lead with the outcomes they care about. Work back to details like integrations and permissions.
  Keep it interactive: *"What stands out to you first?"* (Cory Bray).
- 📌 **Mixtape demo.** Don't send a 60-minute recording. Send short clips they can forward internally.
- 🛠 RAMPED demo flow (45 min):

| Minutes | Step |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | PPO; confirm the 3 problems from discovery and add anything new |
| 5–10 | Reverse demo: "Show me how you do this today" |
| 10–30 | Show 3 workflows, one per problem. After each: "How would that change things for you?" |
| 30–35 | Recap in their words; hand them the 60-second internal pitch |
| 35–45 | 5-Minute Drill + close plan review + book the next-next step |

## 2.5 Multithreading and getting to power

- 📌 **Labeling statement** (Maddy Jackson, #212): *"I often hear {title} likes to weigh in — is that
  the same for you?"*
- 📌 If you have leverage but haven't reached the decision-makers, **make access to power a condition**
  before negotiation moves forward.
- 📌 When you ask for a senior intro, **tie it to how their involvement helps the project** (Kyle Asay, #145).
- 📌 **Get exec intros through your champion.** Map where the real power sits and turn your champion
  into an advocate across the org (Christine Nolan, #537).
- 📌 **Brief your exec** before they join a call. Have them ask about the buyer's business challenges
  and *"what does success look like"* (Caitlin Zylstra, #205).
- 📌 Watch group meetings to spot the **shadow decision-maker**.
- 📌 In a straight head-to-head against a competitor, tip the odds with **onsites, power, and
  references**. About half of deals are won by simply being the better seller.
- 🛠 **Stakeholder map** (template in [`04`](04-templates-and-scorecards.md#stakeholder-map)). Every
  deal above $X needs at least 3 contacts engaged before S3, including the economic buyer.

🛠 Ask for power through your champion:
> "To make sure we're not wasting your time — on deals like this, {CFO/CISO} usually wants a say on
> {their concern}. Rather than you carrying that, would it make sense for me to get 20 minutes with
> them so you're not stuck answering questions you shouldn't have to? I'll prep you on exactly what
> I'd cover."

## 2.6 Building champions

- 📌 *"Just because someone is taking a first meeting with us, that doesn't make them automatically a champion"* (Krysten Conner).
- 📌 *"Don't sell to your champion. Sell THROUGH them"* (#554).
- 📌 Tie the champion's personal win to the business case (Joe McNeill, #227).
- 📌 **Screenshot references.** Line up references by checking in with past customers *before* you
  urgently need them (Krysten Conner, #543).
- 🛠 **Champion test.** A champion does all three:
  1. Has power or influence.
  2. Personally wins if this deal happens.
  3. Has taken an action for you, e.g. an intro, sharing internal info, or setting a meeting.

  If they haven't, they're a coach, not a champion.

## 2.7 The business case

- 📌 Nate Nasralla (Fluint, #117): build the business case in **"Mad Lib style."** Start from a framework
  and fill in the blanks *during the call*.
- 📌 A one-page business case answers three things:
  1. The problem.
  2. The cost of inaction.
  3. Why now.

  Write it in the **buyer's language**. Your champion should feel they wrote it, because they'll
  defend it in rooms you're never in.
- 🛠 Template: [`04 → Business case`](04-templates-and-scorecards.md#one-page-business-case).

## 2.8 The close plan (mutual action plan)

- 📌 **Customer-facing close plan** that covers the key gates: **power, security, legal**. Review it
  **live on every call**; don't email it once and forget it (Jeremey Donovan, #111).
- 📌 Map the key milestones and customize them for each prospect (Ross Rich, #116).
- 📌 Use the MAP to hold the prospect accountable, and be direct (Tom Hemmingsen, #92).
- 🛠 Template: [`04 → Close plan`](04-templates-and-scorecards.md#close-plan--mutual-action-plan).
  Work backwards from the buyer's go-live date.

## 2.9 Negotiation and pricing

📌 30MPC Negotiation Playbooks (Playbook 3, Playbook 13/14, Hall of Fame #581):

**Before you negotiate**
1. Know **your needs and wants** and **their needs and wants**.
2. **Lock in access to power.** Confirm the person you're negotiating with can get the deal done on time.
3. Confirm **you're the vendor of choice**. Then lock the **timeline**. Only then trade.
4. Set expectations up front, e.g. *"we don't do end-of-month discounts,"* before you need to. Agree
   in the upfront contract that pricing comes **after discovery**.

**When you give price**
5. *"Explain how price is structured BEFORE you give it, then shut up."*
6. Tell them in the agenda that you'll share price before the call ends (Belal Batrawy).
7. Set up pricing options around **what they want**, not around your units such as seats.
8. **Decoy pricing:** three options, e.g. $6 (undesirable) / $7.50 (acceptable) / $8 (just what they need).
9. Don't hide the price or wait to give a range; delaying it kills trust (Todd Caponi, "7 Deadly Sins").

**When they push**
10. **Flinch and trade.** Act surprised at a discount request, and **never concede without a give-for-get**.
11. Package **price, terms, and conditions together** so the buyer weighs real trade-offs instead of
    a stream of separate asks.
12. **Caponi's four levers:**
    - Volume
    - Timing of cash
    - Length of commitment
    - Timing of the deal

    Share them *early* so your champion can defend the trades internally.
13. Get a firm commitment on timing **before** any discount. If the deal slips, pricing goes back to
    standard (Will Padilla, #129).
14. Know when competitors' fiscal periods end, so you can predict when they'll drop price (Johnny Larson).

🛠 Discount request talk track:
> "Hm — that's a big ask *(pause)*. I want to get this done for you, so let's look at what we can
> trade. If we can get signature by {date}, a {2-year} term, and annual upfront, I can take that to
> my team. If the date slips, we go back to standard pricing. Which of those can you do?"

## 2.10 Forecast hygiene and deal qualification

- 📌 Point it out when a deal no longer looks like a top priority. Bring in your execs when you're
  dealing with theirs. Show your research early (Andy Whyte, #87).
- 📌 Test deals against **binary yes/no criteria** (Lightning Pipeline Review, Module 3).
- 🛠 Use these forecast categories, defined by stage exit criteria rather than gut feel:

| Category | Rule |
|---|---|
| Commit | S5 reached; close plan dated through signature; economic buyer has agreed value; no unresolved red flags |
| Best case | S4 reached; business case in progress; power met |
| Pipeline | S1–S3 |
| Omit | No next step on the calendar, or buyer stopped responding for 14+ days |

## 2.11 Closing do's and don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Define stages by buyer agreements | Count meetings as progress |
| Run the 5-Minute Drill on every call | End a call without a dated next step |
| Book next-next steps | Give a free 60-minute demo with no process |
| Get to power before negotiating | Negotiate with someone who can't sign |
| Co-write the business case | Send a generic ROI calculator |
| Review the close plan live | Email the MAP once and forget it |
| Explain price structure, give price, stop talking | Fill the silence after stating price |
| Flinch, then give-for-get | Discount without a commitment |
