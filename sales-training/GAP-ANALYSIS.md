# Gap Analysis — Ramped Sales Playbook v4.1 vs. 30MPC Frameworks

**Compared:** *Ramped Sales Playbook* (October 2026 edition, 15 chapters + 2 appendices) against the
30MPC-based manual in this folder (Modules 0–4).
**Question:** Where does the Ramped playbook fall short of the structure and best practices 30MPC teaches?

---

## Bottom line

The Ramped playbook is already a strong document. In several areas it goes further than the 30MPC
material, and those areas should not be changed:

- **Product, legal and safety guardrails:** "Say this, not that", Texas § 250.010, ASTM wording, and opt-outs.
- **Enterprise selling:** the Land → Prove → Program sequence, the vendor onboarding checklist, and the HSE veto.
- **Forecasting:** Forecast Court, Red/Green reviews and the Lookback.
- **CRM:** a field-by-field implementation.
- **Call delivery:** coaching on tone and pauses.
- **Next meetings:** BAMFAM (book the next meeting before the current one ends).
- **Rep economics:** compensation and the career ladder.

It also already uses 30MPC's cold-call research: the Gong opener data and the *Cold Calling Sucks* figures.

The gaps fall into three groups:

1. **The middle and end of the deal are thin.** Negotiation is one paragraph. There is no customer-facing
   close plan on standard deals. Calls have no agenda opener and no fixed closing ritual. Demo technique is
   missing. The deal stages are mostly seller and operations milestones, not things the buyer has agreed to.
2. **Outbound is underbuilt.** The sequence is 5 touches over 21 days, against roughly 10 over 30. The
   cold-call pitch leads with features. The cold email asks for a meeting on the first touch. There is no
   messaging per persona and no video channel.
3. **Leadership and measurement are thin.** Every scorecard target says "TO CONFIRM". There are no
   certification rubrics, nothing on hiring or interviewing, and the ramp is short with no buddy and no
   low-stakes "training leads".

Severity key: 🔴 high (costs deals or pipeline now) · 🟠 medium · 🟡 low / polish.

## 2. Running the deal and closing

| # | Area | Ramped playbook today | 30MPC practice | Gap | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Negotiation** (Ch 10 "Permits, approvals and paper") | One line: "Negotiate with give-gets," with examples. No negotiation chapter. Price guidance is limited to "do not quote a number you cannot back." | A full playbook. Lock in power, vendor of choice and timeline **before** trading. Explain how pricing is built, give the price, then go silent. Flinch, then trade. Name the levers early (Caponi's four). Package price and terms together. A **slip clause**: if the date slips, price goes back to standard. Never hide the price range (Caponi's "7 deadly sins"). Expect end-of-quarter discounts and say up front that you don't do them. | 🔴 | Add a **Chapter 9.5: Negotiation**. See **Section 4** below for a Ramped-specific outline. |
| C2 | **Customer-facing close plan** (Ch 10, 11) | A mutual close plan is required only for pilots and enterprise. Ramped's paper path suits this well: AHJ permit, landlord approval, insurer sign-off, first invoice paid, materials, install slot. Work backward from the buyer's install date. | C3 | **Deal stages** (Ch 10) | 12 CRM stages. Stages 1–6 are mostly activities (Lead → Demo → Multithread → Quote → Agreement). | C4 | **Call opening and closing** (Ch 7, 8) | The cold-call opener is strong, but Demos and Risk Assessments have **no agenda-setting step**. | C5 | **Demo technique** (Ch 8, 10) | A "Demo" is defined as a video discovery-plus-solution call. The offer is a 2-minute verbal stack. ## 4. Ready-to-use fixes for the 🔴 items

### Script 1: Problem proposition (replaces Ch 6 beats 4–6)

> **4. Problem (two options, vivid, true):** "The reason I'm calling: [owners of equipment yards] usually
> tell me one of two things — either they're **still losing equipment and copper at night even with
> cameras up**, or they're **paying for a night guard who can't see the whole lot**."
>
> **5. One-line differentiator:** "We put in a perimeter that deters at the wire, alarms on a cut or climb,
> and runs on its own power."
>
> **6. Interest question:** "Are either of those on your radar?"
> → *Yes:* "Which one?" (one quick discovery question) → "Worth a 20-minute video Demo so I can see your
> site? I've got Tuesday at 10 or Thursday at 2."
> → *No / brush-off:* Cold-call Miyagi: acknowledge → clarify → wax back.

Why this is better:
- Two problems give the buyer something to pick from.
- The differentiator stays in the spec's own words: "deters at the wire", "alarms on a cut or climb".
- Saying "Are either of those on your radar?" earns the Demo ask instead of jumping straight to it.

### Script 2: Voicemail (replaces App. A)

> "Hi [Name], [Your name] with Ramped Security. Calling about [specific signal] at [property]. I'm
> sending you a short note with the subject '[subject]' — take a look there. Thanks."

### Section 3: The 5-Minute Drill (add to the end of every Demo and Risk Assessment, before BAMFAM)

> "We've got five minutes — can I be direct? Based on what you shared, is [stacked pain] something you
> want to fix, or more of a nice-to-have? … When does it need to be solved by, and what's driving that —
> the insurance renewal, the guard contract? … Walk me through how a project like this gets approved —
> the last time you put money into the perimeter, who signed and what were the steps? … Great — so the
> next step is [X], and after that [Y] with [economic buyer]. Let's book [X] now."

### Section 4: Negotiation chapter outline (Ramped-specific)

1. **Before price: lock three things.**
   - **Power:** the economic buyer has been met, and you've confirmed who signs on their side. Ramped's President signs on ours.
   - **Vendor of choice:** "If price weren't a factor, is Ramped the one you'd pick?"
   - **Timeline:** the date the first invoice will be paid. This is the real close date under Net 30.
2. **Set expectations early.**
   - Say in the Demo agenda that you'll share a ballpark before the call ends. Hiding the range kills trust.
   - Say up front that pricing is set by the site assessment and that we don't discount at the end of the month.
3. **Deliver price cleanly.**
   - Explain how it's built: perimeter length, energizers, solar and battery sizing, signage.
   - Present both lanes (Full-Perimeter or Phased) and both structures (own or lease).
   - State the number, then go silent and count to eight. This uses the pause the playbook already teaches.
4. **Name the levers early** so the champion can defend trades internally. Ramped's levers:
   - Own or lease
   - Full-Perimeter or Phased scope
   - First-invoice timing, which pulls the install slot forward
   - Lease term or maintenance-plan length
   - Number of sites or waves
   - Reference visit or case study rights
5. **When asked for a discount:** flinch, then trade.
   - "That's a big ask. Let's see what we can trade."
   - Never give a concession without getting something back.
   - Bundle the asks so you aren't negotiating one item at a time.
6. **Slip clause.** Every quote has a validity date. If the signature or first-payment date slips, pricing goes back to standard.
7. **Authority.** Write down which discounts a rep can approve and which go to the RSD or President. Margin floor per Ch 11.

## Where Ramped is ahead (do not regress)

- **Ethics, compliance and claims discipline**: the six rules, opt-out vs. objection, "Say this, not that", recording disclosure. 30MPC has no equivalent.
- **Enterprise track**: Land → Prove → Program, paid first site, vendor onboarding from the first call, HSE veto, 75% capacity cap. This is more complete than anything in 30MPC's public material.
- **Forecasting**: Forecast Court, Red/Green, close date set to first-payment date, the Lookback.
- **Discovery recap/label/confirm and "stack the pain"**, plus **looping** for deal objections.
- **Delivery coaching**: words per minute, pitch, the three pauses, the pause after the ask.
- **BAMFAM, the end-of-day checklist, the Six Laws, and CRM implementation detail.**
- **Rep economics and the career ladder.**
